Lend me a Feather, so I can Write a Word

2026

One of the oldest known recordings of a human voice is Édouard-Léon Scott de Martinville singing Au clair de la lune in 1860, traced as a line on soot-blackened paper and not heard until the paper was scanned in 2008. The song goes on prête-moi ta plume, pour écrire un mot (lend me your feather, so I can write a word), but his recording breaks off just before he says plume, the feather.

This work supplies the missing word. The two syllables, plu and me, sung in my own voice, are cut by hand into the edges of two feathers. A flatbed scanner reads the cuts and turns them back into sound, each syllable heard while the scanner’s light travels along its feather.
