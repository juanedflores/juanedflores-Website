The Air Is a Library

2026 · In progress

I don’t want what I say to be heavy and I don’t want it to fly away. A feather is just enough.

Every recording medium is a bet about what a material can remember. This work tests an unlikely one: can a feather hold a voice?

A recorded ahhh is reduced to a single vibration, about 400 samples long. Each sample becomes the width of a cut in the vane, made by hand with calipers and a scalpel along the feather’s 200 mm. The feather keeps the voice’s timbre but not its time: one vibration, which only an apparatus can make sound again.

The installation in development places the feather on a flatbed scanner with its lid open. The scanner’s light bar passes slowly under the vane, software measures the cut edge, and the voice is heard only while it is being read.
